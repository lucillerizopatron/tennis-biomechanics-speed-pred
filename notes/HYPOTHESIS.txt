Hypothesis

Note: this was formed after our preliminary runs, which already suggested it. It
was written down before the full experiments, and reorganised around one central
story after feedback from our supervisor. The full experiments are meant to test
it properly, with player-level confidence intervals.

Central question
Which traits are carried by the serving arm alone (3 joints), and which need the
full body (17 joints)? In other words, what does a reduced skeleton encode in
tennis serves?

The story
Our four targets differ in what kind of information they leave in the skeleton.
- Stable, between-player traits (gender, player identity) are the same on every
  serve a player hits, so body shape and movement style can carry them. We expect
  pose to predict them.
- Momentary, within-player traits (serve speed, serve number) vary from serve to
  serve for the same player. Serve speed is mostly explained by who is serving
  (gender, height), which the metadata already holds, and serve number is a
  within-player difference. We do not expect pose to add anything to the metadata.
- Among the stable traits, gender is a coarse two-class trait, so we expect the
  serving arm to carry much of it. Identity is fine-grained, so we expect the full
  body to be needed.

Predictions
- Gender: the serving arm is about as informative as the full body, and the signal
  is not just limb length.
- Player identity: the full body is clearly better than the arm, and much of it
  may be body shape.
- Serve speed and serve number: neither input carries them beyond gender, serve
  number and height.

Setup
- Splits: by player (gender, speed, serve number); by match for identity (only
  players seen in two or more matches).
- Inputs: serving arm vs all 17 joints; shape only (bone lengths), raw motion
  features, motion features scaled by limb size, and combinations. This separates
  body shape from movement.
- Baselines: majority class, and gender + serve number + height (+ speed where
  it is not the target).
- Models: Random Forest on summary features; small lightly tuned transformer on
  raw positions.
- Uncertainty: player-level bootstrap confidence intervals.
- Speed result: checked against pipeline problems (smoothing cutoff, scaling,
  handedness table) before reporting it as a property of the data.
